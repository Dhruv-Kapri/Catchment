Catchment — Reading List

A curated, verified reading list — to turn open data into a demand map, a reach table and a defensible plan for where the next dark stores should go.

Companion to the Catchment project · Oct 2026 · every link verified against primary sources · old foundations + current (2023–26) work

How to use this. You don't need all of it. Read §1–2 for stage 0 (demand), §3 for stage 1 (reach), and §4–5 for stage 2 (the CP-SAT plan and its greedy baseline). §8 is the reference for stage 3 (API and map), §6–7 give the domain and the industry context and the dated store counts for the competitor layer, §9 is a lookup table, and §10 is for the later ops simulator. Operations research, retail geography and q-commerce are the new ground here, so §4 and §6 come first within their stages; you already know CP-SAT mechanics, GIS and MapLibre, so those entries are short.
Suggested path (≈4 weeks of evenings, alongside the roadmap): Week 1 — §1 H3 and MAUP, §2 WorldPop, night lights and Overture, while building stage 0 → Week 2 — §3 OSRM, OSM quality in India and the travel-time error papers, for stage 1 → Week 3 — §4 Church & ReVelle, Huff and the competitive-location overview, then §5 the CP-SAT primer and greedy guarantees, for stage 2 → Week 4 — §8 serving stack, §6 for sourcing competitor counts, §7 for one engineering post, then §10 when the first cut ships.

1 — H3 & Spatial Indexing (the unit demand is counted in)

H3: Uber's Hexagonal Hierarchical Spatial Index — Brodsky, Uber Engineering blog (2018) The original statement of why hexagons (one neighbour distance, 12 pentagons, approximate parent-child nesting); read it before you pick the working resolution. uber.com/blog/h3/
H3 Documentation: Introduction and Resolution Table — Uber / h3geo.org Cell areas and edge lengths per resolution, plus the traversal and polyfill calls you will use to hex the Gurugram + South Delhi polygon. h3geo.org/docs
Rectangular and Hexagonal Grids Used for Observation, Experiment and Simulation in Ecology — Birch, Oom & Beecham, Ecological Modelling 206(3-4) (2007) paywalled The neutral, non-Uber argument for hexagons (uniform adjacency, less edge bias) and where squares still win; it backs your CONTEXT.md choice of "hex". doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2007.03.041
The Modifiable Areal Unit Problem in Multivariate Statistical Analysis — Fotheringham & Wong, Environment and Planning A 23(7) (1991) paywalled Shows that results change with zone size and shape; the reason to test two H3 resolutions and report how the plan moves, not just pick one. doi.org/10.1068/a231025

2 — Building a Demand Surface (population, affluence, activity; every factor a labelled assumption)

WorldPop, Open Data for Spatial Demography — Tatem, Scientific Data 4 (2017) What the WorldPop grids are, how they are produced and what their uncertainty is; the base of your demand formula. doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2017.4
Disaggregating Census Data for Population Mapping Using Random Forests with Remotely-Sensed and Ancillary Data — Stevens, Gaughan, Linard & Tatem, PLOS ONE 10(2) (2015) The method behind WorldPop's top-down grids; explains why 100 m cells are modelled, not counted, so hex totals inherit that error. doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0107042
Spatially Disaggregated Population Estimates in the Absence of National Population and Housing Census Data — Wardrop, Jochem, Bird, Chamberlain et al., PNAS 115(14) (2018) The bottom-up counterpart to Stevens; useful for judging how far to trust any single population layer. doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1715305115
Generating Surface Models of Population Using Dasymetric Mapping — Mennis, The Professional Geographer 55(1) (2003) paywalled The classic dasymetric idea: redistribute counts onto the cells where people actually live; you do a version of this when you weight population by buildings. doi.org/10.1111/0033-0124.10042
Annual Time Series of Global VIIRS Nighttime Lights Derived from Monthly Averages: 2012 to 2019 — Elvidge, Zhizhin, Ghosh, Hsu & Taneja, Remote Sensing 13(5) (2021) The method paper for the VIIRS product you use as an affluence proxy, with its filtering and its limits. doi.org/10.3390/rs13050922
Which Night Lights Data Should We Use in Economics, and Where? — Gibson, Olivia, Boe-Gibson & Li, J. Development Economics 149 (2021) paywalled Compares VIIRS and the older DMSP lights as a stand-in for economic activity, and says where each breaks down, notably at fine scale and in dense cities. doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2020.102602
Microestimates of Wealth for All Low- and Middle-Income Countries — Chi, Fang, Chatterjee & Blumenstock, PNAS 119(3) (2022) The Meta Relative Wealth Index method, a satellite-and-ML affluence layer to compare your night-light proxy against (data licence still unchecked). doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2113658119
Overture Maps Places Guide — Overture Maps Foundation Schema, categories and confidence scores for the places layer; read before choosing which categories count as commercial activity and as candidate-site evidence. docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places/

3 — Road Networks & Travel Time (reach: how long a rider really takes)

Contraction Hierarchies: Faster and Simpler Hierarchical Routing in Road Networks — Geisberger, Sanders, Schultes & Delling, WEA 2008 paywalled The algorithm OSRM's fast mode is built on; explains why a site-by-hex matrix is cheap to compute after a one-off preprocessing step. doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-68552-4_24
Real-Time Routing with OpenStreetMap Data — Luxen & Vetter, ACM SIGSPATIAL (2011) paywalled The OSRM paper itself: what it computes, what it ignores (live traffic), and how it is fed by OSM. doi.org/10.1145/2093973.2094062
Route Planning in Transportation Networks — Bast, Delling, Goldberg, Müller-Hannemann et al., arXiv (2015) The survey of speed-up techniques (CH, hub labels, and others); read the sections on one-to-many and matrix queries for your travel-time table. arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140
OSRM Profiles — Project OSRM (GitHub docs) How a profile turns OSM tags into speeds and penalties; this is where a two-wheeler profile and its default speeds live, and what you would edit. github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/blob/master/docs/profiles.md
OpenStreetMap Data as a Basis for Routing: How Well Does It Really Work? — Herfort & Würzler, HeiGIT write-up of a FOSSGIS 2026 talk (Apr 2026) Reports that max-speed tags are only about 1.9% complete on one Indian corridor, so engines fall back on defaults; a direct warning for free-flow times in India (talk write-up, not peer-reviewed). heigit.org/openstreetmap-data-as-a-basis-for-routing-how-well-does-it-really-work/
Study of Travel Time Variability Using Two-Wheeler Probe Data: An Indian Experience — Chepuri, Wagh, Arkatkar & Joshi, Proc. ICE Transport 171(4) (2018) paywalled GPS-instrumented two-wheeler runs in Surat; one of the few Indian two-wheeler travel-time datasets, a sanity check for your congestion multiplier. doi.org/10.1680/jtran.16.00101
A Comparison of Travel Time Data from Open Source Routing Machine (OSRM) and Uber — Ghanem & Rabalert, ACM SIGSPATIAL Workshop on Spatial Intelligence for Smart and Connected Communities (2025) paywalled A direct OSRM-against-traffic-data comparison; the closest published precedent for the spot-check error you will report (title as in Crossref; ACM page is bot-blocked). doi.org/10.1145/3764924.3770898
Using Desktop GIS for the Investigation of Accessibility by Public Transport: An Isochrone Approach — O'Sullivan, Morrison & Shearer, Int. J. Geographical Information Science 14(1) (2000) paywalled The standard reference for network-time catchments rather than circles, which is your brief's central complaint about spreadsheets. doi.org/10.1080/136588100240976

4 — Facility Location: The Classics (what a catchment, a cover and a rival mean)

Optimum Locations of Switching Centers and the Absolute Centers and Medians of a Graph — Hakimi, Operations Research 12(3) (1964) paywalled The origin of the p-median and p-center problems; the baseline vocabulary for "choose N sites on a network". doi.org/10.1287/opre.12.3.450
The Maximal Covering Location Problem — Church & ReVelle, Papers of the Regional Science Association 32(1) (1974) paywalled Your model in its original form: choose p sites to maximise demand within a distance standard; short and readable. doi.org/10.1007/BF01942293
Capacitated Covering Models — Current & Storbeck, Environment and Planning B 15(2) (1988) paywalled Adds store capacity to the covering problem, which is the step from the textbook model to yours. doi.org/10.1068/b150153
Covering Problems in Facility Location: A Review — Farahani, Asgari, Heidari, Hosseininia et al., Computers & Industrial Engineering 62(1) (2012) paywalled A map of covering variants (partial, gradual, backup, capacitated); use it to name what you built and what you left out. doi.org/10.1016/j.cie.2011.08.020
Maximal Coverage Location Problem: Impacts, Significance, and Evolution — Murray, International Regional Science Review 39(1) (2016) paywalled A modern retrospective on MCLP, including its known weaknesses (a hex is either covered or not); good for the honest-limitations section. doi.org/10.1177/0160017615600222
Defining and Estimating a Trading Area — Huff, Journal of Marketing 28(3) (1964) paywalled The Huff model: a customer's chance of choosing a store rises with its attractiveness and falls with distance, divided across all rivals; the cleanest basis for splitting a shared catchment. doi.org/10.1177/002224296402800307
A New Set of Spatial-Interaction Models: The Theory of Competing Destinations — Fotheringham, Environment and Planning A 15(1) (1983) paywalled Fixes a known flaw of gravity and Huff models (clustered stores are not independent); relevant when several of your stores sit close together. doi.org/10.1068/a150015
Static Competitive Facility Location: An Overview of Optimisation Approaches — Plastria, European Journal of Operational Research 129(3) (2001) paywalled Where competitors, market share and cannibalisation enter the location problem, and why competitive models get hard; frames your synthetic competitor layer. doi.org/10.1016/s0377-2217(00)00169-7

5 — Solving It: CP-SAT & Greedy Guarantees (you know CP-SAT; this is for the covering structure and the baseline)

CP-SAT Solver — OR-Tools, Google for Developers The official guide; skim the sections on Boolean indicators and objective bounds, then read solver logs, which tell you the optimality gap of each plan. developers.google.com/optimization/cp/cp_solver
The CP-SAT Primer — Krupke, TU Braunschweig (GitHub) Practical modelling and tuning advice, including how CP-SAT compares with MIP solvers and what its parameters do; use it for the 10-second budget. github.com/d-krupke/cpsat-primer
An Analysis of Approximations for Maximizing Submodular Set Functions, I — Nemhauser, Wolsey & Fisher, Mathematical Programming 14(1) (1978) paywalled Proves greedy reaches at least (1 − 1/e), about 63%, of the optimum for max coverage; the theoretical floor your greedy baseline sits on. doi.org/10.1007/BF01588971
The Budgeted Maximum Coverage Problem — Khuller, Moss & Naor, Information Processing Letters 70(1) (1999) paywalled Greedy guarantees when each site has a cost; the nearest result to capacity-limited siting. doi.org/10.1016/S0020-0190(99)00031-9

6 — Quick Commerce & Dark Stores (the problem domain, from papers and company filings)

Heuristic for Optimisation of Dark Store Facility Locations for Quick Commerce Businesses — Kewalramani & Khadilkar, arXiv 2312.11494 (2023) The roadmap's second baseline: a heuristic for siting stores under a service radius and a daily order cap; a preprint, so read it critically. arxiv.org/abs/2312.11494
Winning the Race to Customers with Micro-Fulfillment Centers: An Approach for Network Planning in Quick Commerce — Yang, Ostermeier & Hübner, Central European J. Operations Research 32(2) (2024) paywalled A peer-reviewed network-planning model for q-commerce; compare its objective and constraints with yours. doi.org/10.1007/s10100-023-00893-x
Mitigating Disruption Impact in Q-Commerce Through Optimization of Dark Store Resilient Portfolio — Raj, Das & Sawik, Transportation Research Part E 205 (2026) paywalled Dark-store selection with disruption and backup stores; relevant to your congestion lever. doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2025.104518
Dark Stores in the City of Light: Geographical and Transportation Impacts of 'Quick Commerce' in Paris — Buldeo Rai, Mariquivoi, Schorung & Dablanc, Research in Transportation Economics 100 (2023) paywalled An empirical look at where dark stores open and how they cluster in a real city, without operator data. doi.org/10.1016/j.retrec.2023.101333
Swiggy Annual Report FY 2024-25 — Swiggy Limited (2025) Primary source for Instamart store counts, store area and contribution margin; every competitor count you use should cite a document like this, with its date. swiggy.com/corporate/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Swiggy-Annual-Report-FY-2024-25.pdf
Swiggy Quarterly Shareholder Letters (e.g. Q4 FY2026) — Swiggy Limited (2024-26) Quarter-by-quarter store counts and unit economics, for the dated figures a synthetic competitor layer needs. swiggy.com/corporate/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Q4-FY2026-Shareholder-letter.pdf
Eternal: Shareholders' Letter and Results — Eternal Limited (Blinkit and Zomato) The index of Eternal's quarterly letters and results, where Blinkit store counts and margins are reported. eternal.com/investor-relations/results/

7 — How Industry Builds This (engineering posts from real companies)

Next-Generation Optimization for Dasher Dispatch at DoorDash — Jin, DoorDash Engineering (Feb 2020) Moves dispatch from one-to-one matching to a mixed-integer program that can build multi-order routes; the origin story for your later rider-batching stage. doordash.engineering/2020/02/28/next-generation-optimization-for-dasher-dispatch-at-doordash/
Using ML and Optimization to Solve DoorDash's Dispatch Problem — Weinstein, DoorDash Engineering (Aug 2021) ML predicts how an order will unfold, then an optimisation layer assigns; shows how predicted times feed a solver, as your travel-time table feeds CP-SAT. doordash.engineering/2021/08/17/using-ml-and-optimization-to-solve-doordashs-dispatch-problem/
The Swiggy Delivery Challenge (Part One) — Gupta, Swiggy Bytes (2018) Swiggy's own breakdown of delivery time into assignment delay, first mile and last mile, in an Indian operating context. bytes.swiggy.com/the-swiggy-delivery-challenge-part-one-6a2abb4f82f6
Pharos: Searching Nearby Drivers on Road Network at Scale — Grab Engineering (2020) Nearest-by-road-time search rather than straight-line distance, at production scale; the engineering counterpart of your OSRM reach table. engineering.grab.com/pharos-searching-nearby-drivers-on-road-network-at-scale

8 — The Serving Stack (API, map, hex layers)

FastAPI Documentation — Ramírez Request models and start-up loading for POST /plan; the lifespan and background-task sections matter for holding the travel-time table in memory. fastapi.tiangolo.com
MapLibre GL JS Documentation — MapLibre You know it from the rooftop project; here you need data-driven fill styling for hex choropleths and layer toggling for two scenarios side by side. maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/
PMTiles Concepts — Protomaps Single-file static vector tiles you already know; relevant if the demand and catchment layers are too big to ship as GeoJSON. docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/
deck.gl H3HexagonLayer — OpenJS Foundation / vis.gl Draws hexes straight from H3 indices, with no polygon geometry to ship; the lightest way to show demand per hex with MapLibre as the base map. deck.gl/docs/api-reference/geo-layers/h3-hexagon-layer
h3-js — Uber (GitHub) H3 in the browser, for cell-to-boundary and hover lookups if you draw hexes with MapLibre alone. github.com/uber/h3-js
PostGIS Documentation — PostGIS project Only if the area grows to all NCR and the travel-time table needs spatial queries rather than in-memory arrays; not needed for the first cut. postgis.net/documentation/

10 — Later: Ops Simulation & Routing (rider batching, VRP and dispatch, for after the first cut)

Algorithms for the Vehicle Routing and Scheduling Problems with Time Window Constraints — Solomon, Operations Research 35(2) (1987) paywalled Origin of the Solomon VRPTW instances; delivery promises are time windows, so this is your benchmark language. doi.org/10.1287/opre.35.2.254
Solomon Benchmark (VRPTW) — SINTEF The instance files and best-known solutions, to test any routing code against before trusting it on your own data (page is bot-blocked). sintef.no/projectweb/top/vrptw/solomon-benchmark/
Hybrid Genetic Search for the CVRP: Open-Source Implementation and SWAP* Neighborhood — Vidal, Computers & Operations Research 140 (2022) paywalled The state-of-the-art heuristic that PyVRP builds on; read it to know what a good rider-routing solver looks like. doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2021.105643
PyVRP: A High-Performance VRP Solver Package — Wouda, Lan & Kool, INFORMS J. Computing 36(4) (2024) A pip-installable VRP solver with time windows; the likeliest drop-in for rider batching in the simulator. doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2023.0055
OR-Tools Routing Library — Google for Developers Routing with time windows and capacities in the tool you already use; a second option next to PyVRP. developers.google.com/optimization/routing
The Meal Delivery Routing Problem — Reyes, Erera, Savelsbergh, Sahasrabudhe & O'Neil, Optimization Online (2018) Defines the dynamic courier-assignment problem on real restaurant-delivery data; the closest open formulation to instant-delivery dispatch (preprint; journal version not confirmed). optimization-online.org/2018/04/6571/
The Restaurant Meal Delivery Problem: Dynamic Pickup and Delivery with Deadlines and Random Ready Times — Ulmer, Thomas, Campbell & Woyak, Transportation Science 55(1) (2021) paywalled Handles uncertainty in preparation times; your picking stage is the same random ready time. doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2020.1000
Meituan's Real-Time Intelligent Dispatching Algorithms Build the World's Largest Minute-Level Delivery Network — Liang, Luo, Duan, Li, Liao et al., INFORMS J. Applied Analytics 54(1) (2024) paywalled A practitioner account of minute-level dispatch at national scale, combining OR and ML; shows what the real system optimises. doi.org/10.1287/inte.2023.0084
SimPy Documentation — SimPy team Process-based discrete-event simulation in Python; the natural engine for orders, picking and riders as event processes. simpy.readthedocs.io

* What's Still Unverified

Company figures: store counts and margins in the Swiggy and Eternal documents were not read in full or checked against each other; reported counts vary by source and date, so record each number with its source and date, as data-sources.md already requires. Individual Eternal letters were not opened; only the results page was confirmed.

arXiv 2312.11494: found only as an arXiv preprint (a Research Square copy also exists); no peer-reviewed version was found.

Reyes et al. (2018): confirmed as an Optimization Online preprint; a journal version was not confirmed. A Transportation Science paper on the same problem is by different authors (Yildiz & Savelsbergh, 2019) and is not listed.

Ghanem & Rabalert (2025): bibliographic data confirmed via Crossref; the study area and findings were not read, so check them before citing the result.

India two-wheeler speeds: no confirmed city-level two-wheeler speed survey for Delhi NCR was found; the Surat study and the OSRM-versus-traffic comparison are the nearest sources, so the congestion multiplier remains an assumption to calibrate by your spot-check.

Meta Relative Wealth Index: the method paper is listed, but the dataset licence has not been checked.
